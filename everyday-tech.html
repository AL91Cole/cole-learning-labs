FOR FAMILIES AND ADULT BEGINNERS

Everyday tech,
one step at a time.

At home, you are the tech team. Follow the clues, try a fix, and show how you know it worked. These activities let you practice that on pretend problems first.

No account is needed. Most activities take 7 to 25 minutes. Everything is practice: the messages and equipment are fictional, so there is no real device to break and the message links stay closed. Go at your own pace and retry as often as you like. There is no time limit or countdown. A small “Active on this page” counter sits under each activity. It stays on your device and is not a score.

Spot a suspicious message

The everyday scenario. A text says a parcel is waiting and asks you to confirm your details today. An email says your account closes in ten minutes unless you sign in from its link.

Why it matters at home. Messages like these work by rushing you. The habit that helps is the same each time: pause, find the clue, and check through a route you already trust.

Fact-check an AI answer

The everyday scenario. You ask an AI assistant about a bus timetable or a shop’s opening hours. It answers at once and sounds sure, but it names no source.

Why it matters at home. An AI tool can state something wrong in a confident voice. The habit that helps: ask where the answer comes from, check that the source exists, and check that it says the same thing.

Fix it step by step

The everyday scenario. The screen is black. The printer sits silent. The computer will not start after you moved the desk. The temptation is to press everything at once.

Why it matters at home. Changing one thing at a time tells you what actually fixed the problem. It also keeps a small problem from turning into a bigger one.

The same method works at home

Every activity above practices three moves: notice the clue, change one thing, check it worked. Here is how they look on real problems.

ProblemNotice the clueChange one thingCheck it worked
A printer that will not printRead what the printer and the computer say: a light, a message, “offline”, a job stuck in the queue.Do one thing, such as unplugging the cable and plugging it back in, or turning the printer off and on. Leave everything else alone.Print one page. If it still fails, write down what you saw and try the next single change.
A text asking for a codeIt is unexpected, it hurries you, and it asks for a code or password that only you should have.Change the route, not the settings. Do not reply or tap the link. Contact the company through the app, bookmark or phone number you already use.Ask through that trusted route whether anything was needed. If nothing was, report or delete the message.
An AI answer with no sourceIt sounds certain but names no source, or names one you cannot find.Ask where the answer comes from, or look it up in one place you trust.Does the source exist, and does it say the same thing? If not, treat the answer as unchecked.

These are everyday habits, not professional advice. For a real account, money or safety problem, contact the company directly, your workplace or school IT team, or someone qualified to help.

Playing with a child

  • Sit together. Share one screen so you can both see the clue.
  • Read aloud. Read the message or question to them, or use the Listen button, which reads the page with your device’s own voice where one is available. It sits in the bottom-left corner and shows as a speaker icon on smaller screens.
  • Let them choose. Let them pick the answer, even when you can see a better one. A wrong choice costs nothing and can be tried again.
  • Ask how they know. “What did you notice?” teaches more than “Is that right?”

Good first choices with a child are Picture patrol and “Find evidence for an answer” above. There are 15 short activities for kindergarten to grade 6 in the K–6 activities. In kindergarten and grade 1, read and play together.

Your privacy

What you type in an activity is not sent to the site. Your work stays in the open page and is gone when you close the tab, unless you choose to save it on the device. Use made-up details rather than real names or passwords. The site does not record audio and has no upload. Like any website, the host receives ordinary connection details, such as your IP address, when it delivers a page. Read the privacy and accessibility information.
