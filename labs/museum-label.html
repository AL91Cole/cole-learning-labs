Printable investigation · Lesson 11

Who Wrote the Museum Label? on paper

Use a separate sheet for your decisions. To print these records and prompts, use Print in your browser.

Open records A–D

  1. Image M-17. Two people stand beside an empty glass case. There is no writing on the picture.
  2. Signed catalog card, March 4. The museum registrar recorded “Willow Museum, 1998. Photographer unknown.”
  3. Unsigned visitor note, March 5. “They remind me of my brothers. I think those two are siblings.”
  4. AI caption draft. “At Willow Museum in 1998, two siblings volunteer beside an empty glass case.” The draft gives no citations.

Draft the label

Make four rows on a sheet for what is visible, the place and year, the family relationship, and the volunteer role. For each row, choose “visible,” “supported by a museum record,” or “hold for confirmation.” Give the source letter, or record that there is no confirming source.

Write a first label using only the evidence you can support. Keep it so you can compare versions. If you are using a printed copy, cover record E until you have a draft.

Open record E after writing the first label

Signed curator update, March 12. “I compared M-17 with our volunteer shift log. It identifies both people in the picture as museum volunteers. Our records give no information about whether they were related.”

Revise and credit the work

  1. Recheck the role and relationship claims. Add a fact only if the new record supports it. In your own words, write a revised label for museum visitors.
  2. Credit Willow Museum, image M-17, and the records you used. The photographer is unknown. Explain who supplied the first draft and who checked the facts.
  3. Give a partner both versions. Have them underline one change they can trace to record E and circle one claim that still needs confirmation.

Take one source-supported correction and one unanswered question back to the lesson 11 handout.
