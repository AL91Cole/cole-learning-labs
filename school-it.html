Purpose and boundaries

The site offers 100 learning activities across grades K–12: 15 K–6 activities, 5 beginner courses, 42 investigations for grades 7–12, 25 game labs, 12 coding paths and Inbox Defender. The investigations use fictional computers, settings, accounts, logs, code, and AI examples. Students inspect evidence, make controlled changes, test, and explain the result. Cybersecurity cases teach authorization, least privilege, remediation, and responsible reporting within the simulation. Browser simulations perform no real terminal execution. Real Linux labs execute commands in a hosted learner machine configured without internet access; they do not authorize attacks against external systems. Requester replies are authored and built into the page. Online replies are not offered in the current public build. The reviewed design, which applies only if a later approved release offers the option, requires the visible AI choice and uses a restricted fictional lab/topic request; typed questions and lab work stay local in that flow. Case evidence and equipment checks are authored or simulated locally.

Classroom collection is locked for privacy review

Independent labs, local report exports, and fictional demonstrations are available. The published site leaves classroom addresses unrouted, so they do not reach the classroom service. The service itself rejects real classroom API requests before reading service configuration or contacting a classroom database or identity service. Hosting settings, a teacher email, a class code, or an enrollment checkbox cannot approve collection. A reviewed code release is required after applicable privacy obligations, school agreements, and operational safeguards are satisfied.

Student and educator information has a defined purpose

Cole Learning Labs does not sell or rent student or educator information, use it for advertising or behavioral profiling, or send lab work for AI-model training. Future classroom records must be limited to a specifically approved educational purpose and authorized recipients, with necessary service providers disclosed and restricted. Aliases and metadata may still identify people; they are included in privacy review.

COPPA, FERPA, New York Education Law §2-d and 8 NYCRR Part 121, and other applicable protections are release requirements. For covered New York school data, this includes the required agency agreement, Parents’ Bill of Rights, security/privacy plan, and supplemental disclosures. Further jurisdictions need their own applicable-law assessment. No completed legal compliance determination, worldwide approval, or NYSED vendor certification is claimed. See NYSED vendor guidance and the current COPPA Rule. Request the current release-review information through the contact below.

Access, retention, and deletion

Use the clear-local-work control before sharing a browser profile. Stopping future saving does not delete an earlier saved copy, and resetting one ticket affects only that ticket. Downloads, clipboard contents, backups, and school submissions need separate handling. The operator cannot recover independent work. No central classroom database is active. A classroom retention policy has not been approved. Necessary purpose/contract-specific deadlines, scheduled erasure, early return/deletion, backups, recovery windows, storage location, transfers, and rights procedures must be settled and tested before real collection. Any future abuse-prevention data collection must also be reviewed.

The operator and school must establish usable privacy and incident contacts, required notices, appropriate authority, processor restrictions, and access/correction/export/deletion processes. Authentication, contract controls, risk assessment, encryption in transit and at rest, trained personnel, and incident response must be evidenced in the actual deployment. Applicable New York requirements reference NIST CSF 2.0 following the 2026 amendment; this site has not completed that assessment. See the official adoption notice. Avoid names, emails, school IDs, passwords, health information, and confidential details in practice notes; an alias does not make that content anonymous.

Classroom link options

A teacher can share either address below, or combine them as https://www.colelearninglabs.com/?mode=student&cart=true. Both only restrict the page that was opened. Neither unlocks an activity, stores anything or sends a request of its own. The page still makes the availability requests listed under Site delivery and protection.

  • Student mode: https://www.colelearninglabs.com/?mode=student hides the portfolio, contact, teacher and sign-in pages and turns off links to other websites. The AI-use notice, the data-practices page and the local report tool stay available.
  • Shared-device mode: https://www.colelearninglabs.com/?cart=true is meant for shared Chromebook carts. The page is never given browser storage, so lab work is not saved, earlier saved work is not offered and display choices are not kept. After 40 minutes without activity the page clears its work and returns to the home page. A warning two minutes earlier lets an active learner keep working. A device woken after that time clears as soon as the page runs again.

These links are a classroom convenience, not a content filter or a device policy. A learner who opens the plain address gets the standard page with optional device saving. Work saved earlier stays in that browser until someone uses the clear-local-work control. Downloaded reports and clipboard contents stay on the device. For enforced separation between classes, use managed-device settings such as ChromeOS ephemeral mode or managed guest sessions, and keep relying on district filtering for web access.

Review the encrypted-file workflow

The school must approve key generation, authenticated public-key distribution, fingerprint checks, authorized key holders, private-key and passphrase custody, recovery, endpoints, and retention before using the optional tool with school records. Only the public .cllpub file is distributed to learners. Private .cllkey files and passphrases must never be attached to Classroom assignments, uploaded to GitHub, or sent in support messages. Students manually submit only the encrypted .cllreport file through their approved assignment system. A lost private key or passphrase cannot be recovered by the site.

Encryption protects attachment contents for the corresponding key holder. It does not conceal Classroom identity, roster membership, submission activity, file size, or information available on a compromised device. Teachers see plaintext after decryption. Authorized school access, parent/eligible-student rights, and legal obligations still apply. The hosting/deployment chain delivers the application code and must remain trustworthy. This is not an independently audited encryption product or a guarantee that only one human teacher can ever view information. Request the data inventory, access model, and key-custody review through the contact below.

Real Linux machines process work remotely

Choosing Start machine opens a hosted Linux machine. The website sends the lab ID, display dimensions, the device’s time zone, session messages, keyboard and pointer input, text the learner pastes into the machine, and work-check requests. Text copied on the machine is placed on the device’s clipboard. The remote machine processes its files and commands and returns screen or terminal output. The controller reads the lab files and recent command history needed to check progress. Anything typed into that machine or saved in its files leaves the browser, including personal information entered by mistake.

Cloudflare relays the connection, DigitalOcean hosts the controller, and the cloud backend uses Docker Cloud Sandboxes for learner machines. Providers process connection and operational metadata under their own applicable terms. The remote screen is the lab machine’s display; the site does not capture the learner’s own screen, other tabs, camera or microphone. Use fictional files and details only; never enter student records, names, contact details, passwords, private keys or confidential school information. Starting a machine is a product action, not parental consent or district authorization.

The service requests machine deletion on Stop, disconnect or session timeout. Network failures, controller restarts or provider errors can delay or prevent cleanup. Provider logs, recovery copies and retention require separate verification; immediate or complete erasure is not guaranteed. Clearing browser work does not erase provider copies. Before school use, review the selected features, required authority and agreements, provider terms, rights procedures and tested deletion with the district. See the School IT review.

Hostnames and browser requirements

The canonical address is https://www.colelearninglabs.com/. The apex colelearninglabs.com redirects to www. Lab images, fonts, styles and application modules use this same canonical website. Real-machine sessions use same-origin /terminal/ws and /terminal/desktop WebSockets, plus health/catalog and viewer-asset requests. HTTPS WebSockets must be allowed for that feature. Cloudflare relays them to the controller; provider access, isolation and deletion require deployment verification. Activity counts, lab telemetry and completion codes are off in the hosting configuration, and the published page does not offer online replies. The server-side reply gateway at /api/requester remains enabled in the production hosting configuration; the published page never calls it. If a later approved release turns one on, it loads the official Cloudflare Turnstile script and frame from challenges.cloudflare.com on demand. An administrator may approve that exact hostname if these optional services are needed; broad wildcard allowances are unnecessary. The invisible check asks no puzzle or checkbox. If blocked, the labs and built-in replies still work and counts are skipped. Turnstile processes browser and connection security signals; see the Turnstile Privacy Addendum. In the reviewed design, optional online requester replies use only the same-origin /api/requester gateway. Its AI Worker is private and reached through a service binding; no public Worker hostname needs to be allowed. The offline character remains available if the service cannot reply. Broad wildcard allowances for GitHub or Cloudflare websites are not needed.

Use supported Microsoft Edge on Windows or macOS and supported Chrome on Chromebooks. JavaScript is required for interactive labs; optional device save/resume needs browser storage. Local encrypted reports require browser cryptography support on HTTPS, with approved file-read and download permissions. School policies can restrict clipboard, file downloads, or cookies. Future connected classrooms should open as full browser pages; strict session cookies and teacher anti-framing controls can prevent embedded sign-in.

Before a lesson, test a representative lab, keyboard/touch interaction, explicit save/resume/clear, text zoom, report copy/download, and optional encryption/decryption on a real managed device and student network. Use fictional test reports and disposable test keys, never real student records. Browser compatibility and filtering approval are separate checks. See Microsoft’s supported Edge platforms.

Checking student downloads

Open My report in a fictional test lab. Review it and confirm approval for an unencrypted copy, then choose Download .txt report. Check the browser Downloads list and open the saved file. Tablets may also offer Save or share .txt with a Files destination. Copy report and Select report text provide alternatives through an approved document app. Saving a file does not submit an assignment. If school policy restricts these actions, use the approved teacher or IT process.

Equipment can be operated with a mouse or trackpad drag, a touch drag grip, or tap-to-pick and tap-to-connect. The rest of the parts tray scrolls normally. Keyboard users can use the large socket controls; no activity requires a precise drag. Test the actual managed device and browser before class.

GoGuardian and district filter review

A website cannot label itself “super secure” in a way that overrides school policy. HTTPS, accurate educational descriptions, and this review page support a district’s decision; access remains under the school’s control.

  1. An authorized GoGuardian administrator can test the exact canonical address and apex redirect in Filtering → Filtering Simulator for the actual student organizational unit and policy.
  2. If the category is incorrect, review Categories → Check URL → Share Category Feedback, or Suggest Category from a browsing record. A category suggestion does not immediately change access.
  3. If approved by the district, the administrator can add the specific website to the applicable policy’s URL allow rules while considering inherited and organizational-unit policies. A teacher’s local setting does not guarantee an override of district filtering.

Security controls and practical limits

Cloudflare supplies HTTPS and network protection; DNSSEC is enabled. The application restricts scripts and network connections with a Content Security Policy, disables unnecessary device permissions, avoids referrer transmission, and uses response-type protection. Optional classroom APIs use server authentication, ownership checks, scoped sessions, request-origin checks, bounded payloads, immutable submissions, and manual grading. These controls reduce risk; they are not a promise that any website is invulnerable.

Browser-delivered website assets are public. Classroom records are not stored in the source repository. Cloudflare’s privacy policy describes hosting and service processing.
