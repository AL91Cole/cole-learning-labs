# Standards connections for teacher planning

Source check: October 20, 2026. This is Mx. Cole’s instructional interpretation of the public fictional labs, not an official crosswalk, certification, endorsement, legal compliance determination or proof that a learner has mastered a standard. A concept connection identifies useful lesson content; it does not establish coverage of every standard within that concept. Districts and teachers select the applicable expectations and assess the actual student work.

## Sources and versions

The [NYSED Computer Science and Digital Fluency page](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/computer-science-and-digital-fluency) supplies the current state standards and grade-band documents. Its five concepts are Impacts of Computing, Computational Thinking, Networks and Systems Design, Cybersecurity, and Digital Literacy. For these secondary labs, NYSED’s relevant bands are 7–8 and 9–12. The [NYSED guidance page](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/computer-science-and-digital-fluency-guidance-resources) supplies implementation materials. NYSED explains that [curricular decisions are local](https://www.nysed.gov/standards-instruction/about-p-12-learning-standards); learning standards do not prescribe a particular curriculum.

CSTA currently publishes the [2026 CSTA PK–12 Computer Science Standards](https://csteachers.org/pk12standards/), with an [official standards browser](https://csteachers.org/pk12standards/view/). This is the publicly available 2026 edition, rather than a claim that the revision is still only forthcoming. Its foundational concepts are Algorithms & Design, Programming, Data & Analysis, Systems & Security, and Computing & Society. The [CSTA explanation of the revision](https://csteachers.org/pk12standards/about/) distinguishes these from the 2017 organization: systems and networking are combined in Systems & Security, and Algorithms & Design is distinct from Programming. AI content is integrated across concepts. The foundational secondary bands are middle school (6–8) and high school (9–12).

Using the 2026 CSTA framework does not assert that New York has replaced its own standards, or that a district has adopted the revised CSTA edition. A district working from 2017 should use that edition’s official materials and make its own crosswalk. This resource does not mix old individual codes with new concept names. No individual standard identifiers or verbatim performance expectations are reproduced. Consult the official materials for their full wording, boundaries and licensing. Citation: Computer Science Teachers Association. (2026). *2026 CSTA PK–12 computer science standards*. https://doi.org/10.1145/3820482.

All five investigation pathways currently offer starting points in lab groups 7–8, 9–10 and 11–12. Those labels describe the lesson’s starting level; they do not replace official standards bands or prove grade-level mastery. Both high-school lab groups belong within the 9–12 standards bands. The table covers the 29 investigations in `src/scenarios.js`; beginner practice and the capstone are separate teaching resources, not additional assessed coverage claimed by this mapping.

## Using the table

The teacher page filters pathway, NYSED concept, CSTA 2026 concept and lab starting grade entirely in the browser. Filters intersect: a row must match every selected value. Selecting a grade refreshes each row’s examples with actual investigations from that band; all five pathways currently offer every starting band. Clear filters restores all five pathways and the original example selection. Native labeled selects, table headers, a caption, a focusable scroll region and an announced result count support keyboard and screen-reader use. No account, external fetch, student record, saved preference or analytics event is needed for filtering.

Choose a lab, read the official grade-band expectation, and decide what evidence would demonstrate the relevant part of it. Review the learner’s explanation and test results rather than treating a completion indicator as a standards assessment. Add a discussion, transfer question or other activity when the selected expectation requires more than the lab supplies. Optional fictional AI chat is not standards evidence, an assessment, or a substitute for reviewing the authored activity.

When learning goals, official frameworks or grade bands change, review this interpretation again. `src/standards.js` keeps the current mapping and links; tests check that mapped examples remain in the right pathway, the real filter combinations behave correctly, and mounted controls can be cleaned up when the route changes.
