# Teaching with Cole Learning Labs

Cole Learning Labs provides 15 browser-based assignments for grades 7–12. It is designed to support demonstration, guided investigation, independent practice, and discussion on school devices, including Chromebooks.

Created by Mx. Cole, the project supports a future goal of teaching Computer Technology through CTE. It should be reviewed against the educator's own learning objectives and student needs before use.

## Choose a starting point

| Band | Suggested emphasis | Useful support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Grades 7–8 | Recognize components and symptoms, make careful observations, explain one decision, and check information | Model an example, define vocabulary, use short written or spoken responses, and allow paired work |
| Grades 9–10 | Compare possible causes, connect settings with outcomes, and support a conclusion with evidence | Ask students to predict a result before acting and justify their next step |
| Grades 11–12 | Investigate less structured problems, weigh alternatives, verify a fix, and communicate a professional outcome | Require an evidence trail, limitations, a verification step, and a clear handoff or security report |

The filters identify an intended level of challenge. They do not prevent a teacher from choosing a different activity or offering additional support. Review the actual wording and task before assigning it.

## Pathways

- **Hardware & ServiceDesk: six assignments.** Use the virtual bench to connect physical observations with troubleshooting decisions. Ask students to explain safe power and connection steps, record the changes they make, and check the original symptom after a repair. A technical fix and delivery to the requester are distinct parts of the support workflow.
- **Network & Operating Systems: three assignments.** Emphasize the relationship between evidence, configuration, and expected behavior. Ask students to explain why a result supports or weakens a possible cause.
- **AI Literacy & Audit: three assignments.** Have students check claims, evaluate evidence, identify missing context, and explain what a responsible user should do next. These are authored AI examples, not a live AI assistant.
- **Ethical Hacking & Penetration Testing: three assignments.** Keep attention on authorization, assigned scope, evidence, risk, remediation, and reporting. Actions and targets are fictional and contained in the simulation.

## A sample 40-minute lesson

1. **Introduce the problem — 5 minutes.** State the objective and ask students what evidence would help. Explain the expected submission and any vocabulary.
2. **Model the process — 5 minutes.** Demonstrate how to inspect a clue, record an observation, and choose a next step without completing the assigned task for students.
3. **Investigate — 20 minutes.** Let learners work independently or in pairs. Ask “What did you observe?”, “What does that suggest?”, and “How will you check?”
4. **Explain and verify — 5 minutes.** Ask for a concise conclusion, supporting evidence, and the result of a verification step.
5. **Export and debrief — 5 minutes.** Copy or download the text report, submit it through the chosen classroom process, and discuss alternative approaches.

Adjust timing for reading needs and task complexity. A short, well-explained investigation can be more useful than rushing through several assignments.

## Assess reasoning as well as outcome

Use the site's educator guidance alongside a rubric appropriate to the lesson. The following is a suggested teacher rubric, not an automatic grading claim.

| Criterion | Evidence to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Observation | Records relevant symptoms and distinguishes observations from assumptions |
| Investigation | Chooses a reasonable test or action and explains why it is useful |
| Decision | Connects the proposed solution to the evidence and considers alternatives |
| Verification | Checks the original problem after acting and reports remaining uncertainty |
| Communication | Provides a clear conclusion, a useful handoff, and appropriate technical vocabulary |

For each criterion, use four levels: **independent and well supported**, **mostly supported**, **developing with prompts**, or **not yet demonstrated**. Discuss an example before students begin. Review reports and reasoning directly; clicking through a workflow or obtaining a completion indicator alone does not demonstrate understanding.

## Classroom setup and accessibility

The lab layout adapts to Chromebook and tablet screens. Use the **PC close-up** view and labeled component selector for small equipment details. Section shortcuts help learners move between the investigation areas without hunting through the full page. Key controls have 44px or 46px minimum heights, with 16px text in form inputs.

Local viewport checks at 1366 × 768, 1024 × 768, and 768 × 1024 found no page-level horizontal overflow. School-device testing remains part of lesson preparation.

- Test one assignment on a managed student Chromebook before teaching. School policies can affect script execution, downloads, clipboard access, or local storage.
- Check keyboard navigation, visible focus, zoom, contrast, and the available alternatives to pointer interactions. The project should not be described as formally accessibility-certified without an audit.
- Give students a written objective and a clear stopping point. Offer vocabulary support, paired discussion, or a spoken explanation when appropriate.
- Give professional portfolio links context before sending younger learners to external websites. External projects have their own interfaces and access requirements.

## Progress, sharing, and reports

The site does not create accounts or send work to a teacher. Progress remains in the current browser profile's local storage for that website address. Another device, a different profile, a different origin, or cleared browser data will not automatically contain the same work.

Students can download a `.txt` report or copy it to the clipboard and submit it through the educator's chosen process. Check the report before submission. If clipboard access is blocked, use the download option. Keep the exported file until receipt is confirmed.

Use assigned browser profiles where available. On shared profiles, have each student export the necessary report and reset the session before the next student begins. Do not rely on local storage as a gradebook or long-term record.

Use fictional names and scenario details. Do not enter real credentials, private student information, or confidential school incidents.

## Boundaries for cybersecurity and AI work

All cybersecurity practice stays within the fictional targets and actions supplied by the lab. Completing an activity does not authorize testing another person, school network, website, device, or account. Any separate real-world exercise needs its own explicit authorization and scope.

Ask students to describe the defensive lesson, recommend a fix, and explain how they would check it. Professional security work includes documenting uncertainty and communicating findings responsibly.

AI scenarios should prompt students to question claims and retain responsibility for their decisions. No live AI service is contacted by these activities. Do not introduce real personal information merely to make an example feel realistic.
