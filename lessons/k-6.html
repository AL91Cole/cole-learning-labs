TEACHER LANE · GRADES K–6

Short technology sessions for grades K–6

Pick one small activity, read it together and talk about how you know. Each session takes about 10 to 20 minutes. Every activity is a fictional picture model: pretend messages, pretend robots, pretend networks.

Who this is for

This page is for the adult in the room: a classroom teacher, a library or technology teacher, an aide, or a family member at home. You do not need a technology background. You read, ask one good question and let the child choose.

The children are the tech team. They look at a picture, make a choice, check it and say how they know. An answer that needs a second try is part of the work.

How to lead it

  • Read the question aloud. Read the choices too. In K–1, read everything. In grades 2–3 and 4–6, read whenever a child asks.
  • Let children point or say the answer. You or a partner can select the choice for a child who points at it.
  • Ask “How do you know?” Ask it after a right answer as well as a wrong one. The reason matters more than the choice.
  • It is fine to retry. Each activity lets a child choose again. Say “Let’s look at the picture once more,” then try another choice.
  • No scores are kept. The activities do not grade or rank anyone, and there is no time limit or countdown. A small “Active on this page” counter sits under each activity. It stays on the device and is not a score.
  • Stay with the pretend world. The names, messages and devices are made up. Children never need to give a real name, password or address.

Ways a child can respond

The activities ask for a choice, not for typed text. Use whichever response fits the child in front of you.

  • Oral

    Works in every band

    The child says the answer and the reason to a partner or to the adult. “I picked the screen because it shows pictures.”

  • Visual

    Works in every band

    The child points at the screen, holds up a numbered card for the choice, or draws what they noticed.

  • Typed

    Only where the child is ready

    A child who is ready can type the closing sentence in a document your school already uses. Typing is never required, and the site does not collect it.

The site does not record audio and has no speech input. A spoken answer is heard by you, in the room.

Listen and Display: two supports on the site

Two small buttons sit in the bottom-left corner of the site. On smaller screens they show as icons only: a speaker for Listen and “Aa” for Display. Try them yourself before the session.

  • Listen reads the page text with a voice built into the device. The text is not sent anywhere. If the device has no built-in voice, the button says so and you read aloud instead. Listen may skip the words on answer buttons, so plan to read the choices yourself.
  • Display changes text size, spacing between lines and words, contrast, and motion (it can reduce animation).

These choices stay on the device. Nothing is sent to the site. On the shared-device link below they last only while the page is open.

Two games that fit the same sessions

  • Inbox Defender: Picture patrol

    Picture patrol · K–6 · about 8 minutes · with a trusted adult in K–1

    Five fictional messages. For each one the child chooses “Pause & get help” or “Use the class bookmark”. Retries are allowed, there is no time limit or countdown, and the message links stay closed. After a correct choice the game names the clue that supported it.

    www.colelearninglabs.com/#play/inbox-defender?style=picture

    Open Picture patrolInbox Defender lesson page

  • Packet Post

    Grades 4–6 · catalog time 10 minutes · a first solve takes about 5

    The player turns path tiles until the route is continuous, then sends a pretend packet to the library. Ask: “Which tile did you turn last, and why did that finish the path?”

Close the session

Finish with two sentences. Children can say them, draw them, or write them if they are ready.

One thing I noticed

Say it, draw it or write it.

One thing I would check next

Say it, draw it or write it.

In a whole group, take two or three answers aloud and stop there. You do not need to collect anything.

Standards connections

These connect to the New York State Computer Science and Digital Fluency Learning Standards (December 2020). The official wording is quoted exactly. The look-for beside it is the author’s wording for what you might see or hear in one session.

ActivityOfficial NYSED statementTeacher look-for (author’s wording, not NYSED’s)
Keep private things private · K–1K-1.CY.1 “Identify reasons for keeping information private.”The child chooses “Pause and ask a trusted adult” for the pretend game’s request and says one reason a real name stays private.
Picture patrol with an adult · K–1K-1.CY.5 “Identify when it is appropriate to open and/or click on links or files.”With the adult reading, the child keeps the surprise picture closed and says the class bookmark is the way to open a planned class task.
Picture patrol · grades 2–32-3.CY.2 “Compare and contrast behaviors that do and do not keep information secure.”The child sorts a pretend message into “Pause & get help” or “Use the class bookmark” and says which choice keeps a name or password safe.

This is the author's own mapping. It is not an official NYSED review or endorsement, and one lesson is practice toward a standard, not proof that a student has met it.

No grades 4–6 code is listed for Inbox Defender. The nearest cybersecurity standard for that band is about applications and devices, not messages. No individual code is listed for Packet Post or for the other activities on this page. The Packet Post lesson page explains why.

Privacy

The K–6 activities need no account, and nothing typed is sent to the site. Everything in them is fictional, and the message links in Picture patrol stay closed.

For a cart or any device more than one child uses, open the shared-device link. It saves nothing on the device and clears the page after 40 minutes without activity. It is a convenience, not a lock: a child can still open the plain address.
