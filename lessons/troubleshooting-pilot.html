PROPOSED PILOT PLAN · EIGHTH GRADE · ONE CLASS PERIOD

Troubleshooting pilot pack for one class period

Two fictional tickets, one method, about 45 to 50 minutes. Pairs solve The Blank Monitor together, then meet a second ticket they have not seen and try the same method with less help.

This is a proposed plan. It has not been run with a class. There are no results to report. If you try it, the checklist at the end is for your own notes.

Time

Purpose and method

The pilot asks one question: after one guided ticket, can students use the same troubleshooting method on a ticket they have not seen? Students practice three moves, in order.

  1. Identify the clue. Name one observation you collected, not a guess.
  2. Choose one change. Change one thing, and say why that clue points to it.
  3. Explain how to verify. Say what you will check to know it worked, including the requester’s original task.

Everything in both tickets is fictional: the requesters, the rooms and the equipment. Students work on a virtual bench and never open a real device.

Before class

  • Open both ticket links on a managed Chromebook, signed in as a student, on the student network. Confirm that each one loads and that the second ticket opens without a membership message.
  • Choose one link mode from the table below and post that link where students already find class links.
  • Decide how reports are handed in through the district’s approved system, for example an assignment in your learning management system. Nothing is submitted by the site.
  • Print one student slip per student for each ticket, or plan to hear answers aloud.
  • Do not preview the second ticket with students. Read it yourself first.

The sequence

Minutes are estimates, and the steps add up to 50. For a 45-minute period, shorten the debrief to 5 minutes. The tickets have no time limit or countdown. A small “Active on this page” counter sits under each ticket. It stays on the device and is not a score. If a pair has not closed a ticket when you move on, they stop where they are and say what they would check next.

  1. Opening demonstration (5 min) Model the three moves on an everyday fictional example that is not about a screen. For example: a pretend classroom speaker has no sound. Think aloud. “First I ask whether the sound stopped after something changed. That answer is my clue. I will change one setting, and I predict what the sound test will show. Then I check that the requester can hear the lesson audio.” Write the three moves on the board: clue, one change, verify. Do not demonstrate either ticket.
  2. Paired investigation: The Blank Monitor (20 min) Pairs open The Blank Monitor. The Operator works the virtual equipment and says the prediction aloud. The Evidence lead records the clue, the one change and the test result. Pairs swap roles after the first test. They work through three pages: Request & clues, Workbench & test, and Check & explain. Circulate and ask “What did you observe?” and “How will you check?” rather than naming a fix.
  3. The unseen second ticket (15 min) Pairs open the second ticket, No Picture After a Presentation, and start in the opposite roles from the ones they began with. Say to students: “Here is a new ticket from a different requester. Use the same three moves. You decide what to check first.” Give less help this time. Answer a question with a question about the evidence.

    Teacher note: the second ticket has the same symptom and a different cause. The ticket itself tells students the cause is different. Do not say what it is.

  4. Debrief (10 min) Bring the class together. Use the three discussion prompts and the exit question below. Students hand in their slip or report through your usual route.

Debrief prompts

  1. Which clue did you use on each ticket? Say it the way you recorded it.
  2. How can the same message on the screen have a different cause this time?
  3. How did you check that the requester’s original task worked, and not only that the screen changed?

Exit question

A different computer also shows no picture. What evidence would you collect before choosing the same repair?

What each student should produce

For each ticket, three things in the student’s own words: the clue they used, the one change they chose, and how they verified it. Accept any of these.

  • Orally. The student explains aloud to you. A student may also use the device’s own district-approved dictation to type into the same text boxes. The site does not record audio and has no speech input.
  • On the printed slip. Three boxes, below.
  • In the report. In the ticket, My report opens a preview that begins with a four-line summary: Clue, Change, Result, Next step. The student reviews it for private details and ticks the review box, then copies it or downloads a .txt file and hands it in through the district’s approved system. Nothing is submitted automatically.

Look-fors

These are the same three levels as the Blank Monitor lesson. They describe the quality of the reasoning, not whether the ticket closed, and they do not list a repair. Use the same look-fors for a spoken, dictated, handwritten or typed explanation: a student may explain aloud to you, or use district-approved dictation on the device. The site does not record audio. A passed check in the simulation is evidence about the simulation. It is not a grade.
