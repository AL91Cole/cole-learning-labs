TEACHER-LED ACTIVITY · GRADES 9–12 · PRINTABLE TEMPLATE

Design a fictional troubleshooting ticket

A teacher-led activity for older students. Pairs invent a problem, hide its cause, and leave the clues another pair needs to find it. Everything in the ticket is fictional.

Go to the templateAll teaching pages
Time
About 30–45 minutes
Who
Older students, grades 9–12, in pairs
Materials
One printed template per pair, or a document in a tool your school already uses
Led by
The teacher. Nothing is done on this site.

For the teacher: why design a ticket

Writing a good ticket shows understanding of cause, evidence and verification. To write one, a pair has to decide what really went wrong, which observations would reveal it, which observation rules out the tempting wrong answer, and how a solver would know the fix worked. That is the same method the labs ask students to follow: clue, one change, verify.

Printing this page gives the student handout only: the ground rules, the template, the peer swap and the hand-in note. These teacher notes are left out of the printout.

How to run it

  1. Set the rules and show the parts (5 min) Read the ground rules aloud. Show the six parts of a ticket with a short example of your own, such as the one below. Say which part is kept from the solver.
  2. Draft the ticket in pairs (15–20 min) Pairs fill in the template. Start with the hidden cause, then work outward to the symptom and the clues. Circulate and ask “Which clue rules out the obvious wrong answer?” As pairs finish, check each ticket against the Teacher review list before another pair sees it.
  3. Peer swap (5–10 min) Once you have reviewed a ticket, another pair solves it on paper and gives two sentences of feedback.
  4. Revise and hand in (5–10 min) Pairs change one thing in response to the feedback, then hand the ticket in through your usual route.

A short example to show the class

An invented requester, Mr. Okafor in Room 12, reports that a tablet will not charge. The hidden cause is that the charging cart’s power switch is off. One clue rules out a tempting wrong cause: the same cable charges a different tablet at the wall, so the cable is not broken. The safe test is to turn the cart switch on. The expected result is that the charging light comes on, and Mr. Okafor can open the reading app once the tablet has some charge.

A spoken option

A pair may present the ticket aloud to you instead of writing every box. If a student needs typed text, the device’s own district-approved dictation can type into a document your school already uses. The site does not record audio and has no speech input. Use the same Teacher review list either way.

Ground rules: fictional only

  • Invent every name, room and device. Use made-up requesters such as “Ms. Vale, Room 9”.
  • No real people, passwords, addresses or school systems. Do not describe a real problem at your school.
  • Write the requester with respect. The ticket is about the equipment, not about a person’s mistake.
  • The solver must be able to find the cause from the clues. A ticket that cannot be solved is not finished.

The ticket template

Designed by (as your teacher asks):

Ticket title and fictional requester

A short title, and an invented person and place.

Symptom

What the requester notices, in their words. Describe what they see or hear, not what caused it.

“…”

Hidden cause

What is really wrong. This is kept from the solver until the end.

Clues

Three or four observations a solver could collect. At least one must rule out a tempting wrong cause. Mark that one with a star.

Clue 1 Clue 2 Clue 3 Clue 4 (optional)

Safe test

One change the solver can try that cannot damage anything or expose data.

Expected result

What the solver should see if the idea is right, and how they confirm that the requester’s original task works.

Teacher review

Your teacher checks the ticket before another pair tries it, and ticks each line that is true.

  • Fictional only: no real people, passwords, addresses or school systems.
  • The cause is discoverable from the clues.
  • One change at a time is enough to solve it.
  • The test is safe.
  • The requester’s original task is verified, not only the first sign of success.
  • The wording is respectful.

Teacher comment

Peer swap

  1. Wait until your teacher has reviewed your ticket. Then keep your sheet face down, and read the title and the symptom aloud to another pair.
  2. The solving pair says what they want to inspect. Each time, read out a clue that fits. Do not read the hidden cause.
  3. The solving pair uses the method: clue → one change → verify. They name the clue they would use, the one change they would make, and how they would verify it.
  4. Turn the sheet over. Compare their answer with your hidden cause and expected result.
  5. The solving pair gives two sentences of feedback.

Feedback from the solving pair

One clue that helped us was … One thing that would make this ticket clearer is …

How to hand it in

Print this page and write on it, or save a copy locally and type under the same headings in a document your school already uses. Then submit it through your district’s approved classroom system, the way your teacher asks.

Your ticket stays with you and your teacher, and nothing is uploaded to this site. There is no public gallery and no submission form. The site does not publish student tickets.

Optional extension: compare with a lab ticket

Open a ticket from the labs, such as The Blank Monitor (Grades 7–8 in the catalog, about 15 minutes, and a fair comparison for older students too). Find its symptom, its clues and its check of the requester’s original task. Then ask each pair: which part of your ticket is as clear as the lab’s, and which part would you rewrite?
